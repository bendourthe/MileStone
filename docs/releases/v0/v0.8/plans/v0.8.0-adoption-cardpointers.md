**Goal**: Build a maintained, provider-independent library of institutions, reward programs and card products beyond Amex and Chase, let a person add a newly acquired card from another provider without waiting for an authenticated connector, and compare purchase value locally using sourced rules, user valuations and exact-card offers where supported, while preserving issuer data, sign-ins and Search, then verify and deliver the complete feature.

## Overview

This plan adopts comparison items A1-A4 and the owner-requested broader institution/program/catalog portion of A6 as independent local Android features. CardPointers' code and database are not imported. A5 remains with v0.6 merchant-offer discovery/enrollment; A7 hosted/assistant interfaces remain deferred. The owner confirmed v0.8.0, invoked from-comparison planning and explicitly rejected limiting the library to their current Amex/Chase cards. This request authorizes planning; it does not approve implementation or the comparison's proposed amendments to v0.6/v0.7.

The product library holds public templates and earning rules; the wallet holds specific owned instances. Connected cards retain the existing CardKey. Manual cards use a separate local identity and persistence so issuer inventory pruning cannot delete them. The existing top Search opens a distinct purchase action and its card-library picker. Ordinary keyword Search keeps its accepted added-program/stored-data corpus and exact navigation. Product browsing does not add a program, permit an issuer host, create a sign-in or activate an offer.

The owner requires a growing multi-provider library whose architecture and acceptance do not depend on their present card inventory. Initial publisher research covers current advertised consumer/business reward cards across the US Amex, Chase, Capital One, Citi, Bank of America, Wells Fargo, US Bank and Discover-branded portfolios, plus Canadian RBC, TD, CIBC, Scotiabank, BMO and National Bank portfolios. Resolve brand/legal-issuer distinctions and regional variants from dated official sources. This is an initial coverage floor, not an issuer allowlist or a permanent market ceiling. Preserve existing legacy records without counting artwork as verified earning coverage. New institutions and products must be data additions with stable identifiers, aliases and source review, not a new hardcoded issuer branch. Other countries/providers remain representable immediately through explicit custom records and supported local currencies/units; publisher coverage expands through the same process.

CardPointers' documented pattern is bank/name/region selection, user-created missing cards and reviewed corrections ([card picker](https://help.cardpointers.com/article/75-how-to-add-a-card), [missing cards](https://help.cardpointers.com/article/37-why-dont-you-have-my-card-in-the-app), [correction process](https://help.cardpointers.com/article/38-how-do-i-update-incorrect-information-about-a-card)). Its database export, full sourcing pipeline and maintenance internals are not exposed by the inspected CLI or extension. Use those observed workflows to design MileStone's own institution/program/product registry and reviewed updates; do not claim their 5,000-card inventory is available to import. Public product/rule coverage, owned-card entry, automated balances, automated benefits and offer enrollment are separate capability states for each institution. A new card can be useful for offline comparisons before its bank has an authenticated adapter. No unvalidated connector is represented as working support.

## Goals First / Definition of Done

Target problem: the owner cannot currently answer which owned card returns the most value for a merchant/category and amount without comparing card rules and offers elsewhere. Persona: an Android user with multiple reward cards who wants local control of their wallet and keeps issuer sign-ins on the phone. STRATEGY.md and docs/solutions/ are absent; the owner request, comparison, CLAUDE.md and current source ground this plan.

- [ ] Each supported product has a stable ID, issuer/country/currency/reward unit, dated source and review metadata, base/category rules, caps, exclusions and effective dates. Unsupported/stale data is visible and never becomes a guaranteed winner.
- [ ] The institution/region coverage floor above is inventoried from dated official sources; every currently advertised reward-card product in each frozen source inventory has a product record and reviewed earning rules, or a named blocking unresolved source condition. No source omission is hidden as complete coverage. Preserve existing artwork and legacy recognition independently.
- [ ] Institution, reward program, product and owned-instance IDs are separate. A new provider and its card can be added through data/custom entry without changing issuer enums, the database schema or calculator code. Existing automatic connector capabilities remain accurate and separate.
- [ ] A maintained source registry, bounded collection/validation helper, source-change review and local correction/missing-card workflow make continued growth repeatable. Report coverage by region/institution/product and verification state, never by the owner's captured inventory.
- [ ] A manual owned-card instance can be created, edited, valued and removed offline without creating a bank connection or changing an existing connected card. Duplicate product instances remain distinct.
- [ ] A category/merchant, amount and currency query produces a reproducible, explained comparison across selected owned cards. Required missing inputs yield an actionable incomplete/conditional result rather than invented savings.
- [ ] A confirmed offer counts only for its exact card, qualifying channel/date/threshold and remaining redemption state; expired, redeemed, ineligible, unknown or merely discovered offers do not contribute to confirmed value.
- [ ] Ordinary Search and canonical v0.6/v0.7 destinations retain exact identity and Back/scroll behavior. Viewing, ranking and library edits cause no issuer action or new wallet-data egress.
- [ ] Built-app entry, persistence, migration, recovery, accessibility and privacy checks pass; existing Search p95 remains within its unchanged 200 ms contract. Measure the new workload independently rather than substituting it for the old benchmark.
- [ ] Full/platform/report, current handbook, independent Goal review, owner final acceptance, green integration and separately governed release/archive/native closure complete. Counts and checked tasks alone are not delivery evidence.

Outcome measures: all promised cross-provider reference cases produce the independently calculated order; all disqualifying-offer cases stay out of confirmed totals; a manual-card process-restart flow preserves instance identity and entered data; the existing Search scenario keeps its current latency bound. These are acceptance targets, not results already obtained.

Freshness contract: a product rule is usable without a staleness qualifier only inside its effective dates and for at most 30 elapsed days after its last documented verification. An offer snapshot is usable for the confirmed component only inside its issuer expiry and for at most 24 elapsed hours after a successful exact-card confirmation, with all needed eligibility/progress fields present. These conservative initial freshness limits are product heuristics, not issuer guarantees. Missing/future timestamps, a failed/incomplete refresh, unknown timezone or missing qualifying state make the affected component conditional/incomplete immediately; expiry excludes it. Retain prior data for display but never use preservation as evidence of current eligibility. Boundary tests cover just before/at/after each limit. Results say when the issuer state was last confirmed and remain estimates, not posted savings. Revising these limits later requires a stated product decision and updated tests.

## Scope and Constraints

In scope: multi-provider institution/program/product registries, public earning-rule coverage, a maintainable collection/review/update process, capability-specific provider onboarding, local manual cards, per-card valuations/overrides, deterministic purchase calculation, canonical offer reading, purchase/library UI and final delivery. Existing credits and welcome trackers remain canonical. Authenticated connector expansion gets an explicit evidence-backed onboarding queue per provider, including source hosts, required consent, account coverage and feasibility; enabling a new automated balance/benefit/offer adapter requires its own verified source contract and authorized work. Out of scope for this release's application changes: bank login automation changes, live offer enrollment, household profiles, transaction harvesting, card applications, annual-fee portfolio optimization, location permissions, shopping extensions, cloud sync, hosted LLM/MCP integration and automatic currency conversion.

Use Kotlin/Room and existing Android components; prefer a bundled data file only when it improves reviewed maintenance. Decimal-safe math uses standard-library BigDecimal or integer minor units, with explicit currency scale and rounding. No new runtime service, account or dependency is justified by this plan. Public product records cannot specify executable selectors, network hosts or actions. Keep logs count-only; never print private card names, digits, bindings, balances, purchase inputs, cookies or raw offer text. Public fixtures are synthetic and private captures stay excluded.

MCP Registry Policy: A1-A4 are local-only/re-full; no wrapper to CardPointers is admitted. The issuer is intrinsic to v0.6's action path, but that does not transfer permission to this read-only recommendation feature. Preserve default-on adding on every independently eligible card as the v0.6 owner policy; do not introduce preferred-card enrollment as a calculator side effect.

Input bounds: enforce limits before decimal parsing. Numeric fields accept at most 32 characters and 18 significant digits, ordinary decimal notation only, no exponent/NaN/infinity, and at most six fractional digits before currency-specific scale validation. Purchase amount is positive and at most one billion major currency units; rates and point valuations are nonnegative and at most 1,000 in their displayed units, with a zero valuation explicitly yielding zero value rather than a missing assumption. Cap/usage inputs are nonnegative and within the purchase amount's numeric bound. Merchant, product alias and manual labels are limited to 256 characters; term descriptions to 4,096. Reject out-of-range/oversized edits inline before allocation-heavy parsing, keep the previous valid stored state and never display an obsolete result as applying to the invalid query. Bound local result scans to the admitted workload and report incomplete coverage rather than silently truncate. Test huge pastes, exponent/scale attacks, negative values and conflicting edits.

## Phase 4: Purchase Value Engine

**Goal**: Calculate and explain incremental purchase value with explicit unknowns.
**Prerequisites**: Phases 1-3.
**Stability Gate**: Independent expected-value cases and boundary controls produce deterministic values, qualification labels and stable ties without network or storage mutation.
**Verification Expectation**: Invoke the actual domain service from an emulator integration harness with the synthetic $100 comparison from the seeding report; observe $4.50 on the 3-point/1.5-cent card and $12 on the 2%-plus-qualifying-$10 card, then $1.48 and no fixed credit for the latter at $74. No live issuer terms are implied by these numbers.
**Recommended model tier**: frontier
**Recommended effort level**: max
**Rationale**: Financial units, cap splitting, uncertainty and condition interactions require careful proof.

### Sub-tasks
